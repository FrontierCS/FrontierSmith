# Pentagonal Patrol

## Problem

Nastya has sneaked into the country of Strange Bees again.  
This time the bees are no longer reacting online: they want to publish a **full patrol schedule** in advance.

The beehive is modeled by a connected undirected graph. Each vertex is a chamber, each edge is a tunnel. The graph has the following “pentagonal honeycomb” properties:

- the degree of every vertex is at most 3;
- every edge belongs to some simple cycle of length at most 5.

There are `k` patrol bees. You must choose, for each bee, where it starts and how it moves during the next `H` minutes.

Nastya sees the whole patrol schedule beforehand. She then chooses any initial chamber not occupied by a bee. After that, time proceeds as follows:

- At time `0`, the bees are at their chosen starting vertices, and Nastya is at her chosen starting vertex.
- For each minute `t = 1..H`:
  1. all bees move simultaneously; each bee may either stay or move through one edge;
  2. if a bee moves onto Nastya’s current chamber, Nastya is caught immediately;
  3. otherwise, Nastya must move through exactly one edge to a chamber not occupied by any bee after their move.

Your schedule is evaluated against **all possible choices and movements of Nastya** consistent with these rules.

Each chamber `v` has a honey value `w_v`. A chamber is considered **at risk at time `t`** if Nastya could be in that chamber at time `t` while avoiding capture.

Your goal is to design a patrol minimizing the total honey value of all at-risk chambers over the whole horizon.

---

## Input

The input consists of a single test instance.

- The first line contains four integers  
  `n m k H`  
  — the number of vertices, edges, bees, and minutes.

- The second line contains `n` integers  
  `w_1 w_2 ... w_n`  
  — the honey values of the vertices.

- Each of the next `m` lines contains two integers `u v`  
  — an undirected edge between vertices `u` and `v`.

Vertices are numbered from `1` to `n`.

---

## Output

Output exactly `k` lines.

The `i`-th line must contain `H + 1` integers

`p[i][0] p[i][1] ... p[i][H]`

where `p[i][t]` is the vertex occupied by bee `i` at time `t`.

### Feasibility requirements

Your output is **feasible** iff all of the following hold:

- `1 <= p[i][t] <= n` for all `i, t`;
- for every bee `i` and every `t = 1..H`, either:
  - `p[i][t] = p[i][t-1]`, or
  - there is an edge between `p[i][t-1]` and `p[i][t]`.

Different bees are allowed to occupy the same vertex at the same time.

If the output is infeasible, the score for this test is `0`.

---

## Objective

For each time `t`, let `B_t` be the set of vertices occupied by at least one bee at time `t`.

Define the set `R_t` of vertices where Nastya could be at time `t` without having been caught earlier.

- Initially:

  `R_0 = V \ B_0`

  because Nastya may choose any unoccupied starting vertex.

- For each `t = 1..H`:

  First, Nastya survives the bees’ move only if her previous vertex is not in `B_t`.  
  Let

  `S_t = R_{t-1} \ B_t`.

  Then she must move along one edge to an unoccupied vertex. So

  `R_t = { x not in B_t | there exists y in S_t with edge (y, x) }`.

The **exposure** of your patrol is

`E = sum over t=0..H of sum over v in R_t of w_v`.

Your objective is to **minimize `E`**.

---

## Scoring

Let

- `E` be the exposure of your submission;
- `B` be the exposure of the **baseline patrol** defined below.

The score for this test is

`floor(1,000,000 * min(3, (B + 1) / (E + 1)))`.

So:

- matching the baseline gives about `1,000,000` points;
- doing worse gives less;
- doing better gives more, up to a cap of `3,000,000`.

### Baseline patrol

The baseline is constructed as follows:

1. Choose `k` **distinct** starting vertices greedily.
2. At each step, pick the vertex `v` (not already chosen) that maximizes the total honey value of vertices whose graph distance from at least one chosen vertex in `C ∪ {v}` is at most `2`.
3. Break ties by smaller vertex index.
4. After `k` vertices are chosen, each bee stays forever on its assigned starting vertex:
   `p[i][0] = p[i][1] = ... = p[i][H]`.

The baseline exposure `B` is computed by the same rules as any other patrol.

### Aggregation

If a test file contains multiple instances, the final score is the sum of test scores over all instances.

---

## Constraints

- `4 <= n <= 5000`
- `n - 1 <= m <= 3n`
- `1 <= w_v <= 1000`
- `3 <= k <= min(20, n)`
- `1 <= H <= 4n`
- The graph is connected and simple.
- The degree of every vertex is at most `3`.
- Every edge belongs to some simple cycle of length at most `5`.

- Time limit: 3 seconds
- Memory limit: 512 MB

---

## Example

### Sample input
```text
5 5 2 3
3 1 4 1 5
1 2
2 3
3 4
4 5
5 1
```

### Sample output
```text
1 2 3 4
3 4 5 1
```

### Explanation

There are two bees and `H = 3`.

Their positions are:

- Bee 1: `1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4`
- Bee 2: `3 -> 4 -> 5 -> 1`

So the occupied sets are:

- `B_0 = {1, 3}`
- `B_1 = {2, 4}`
- `B_2 = {3, 5}`
- `B_3 = {1, 4}`

Reachable Nastya positions:

- `R_0 = {2, 4, 5}`, weight `1 + 1 + 5 = 7`
- `R_1 = {1}`, weight `3`
- `R_2 = {2}`, weight `1`
- `R_3 = {3}`, weight `4`

Hence the exposure is

`E = 7 + 3 + 1 + 4 = 15`.

The test score is then computed from this `E` and the baseline exposure `B` for the same instance.
