# Delta Evacuation Ferry

## Problem

A storm is approaching a river delta. Greg has one ferry stationed at the **shelter dock** and must evacuate people from many other docks to safety.

There are `m` docks, numbered `1..m`. Dock `1` is the shelter dock.  
There are `n` people, indexed `1..n`. Person `i`:

- is initially waiting at dock `s_i` (`2 <= s_i <= m`),
- has weight `w_i`,
- has urgency `u_i`.

The ferry:

- starts at dock `1` at time `0`,
- can carry at most `C` people at once,
- can carry total weight at most `K`,
- may move between any two docks.

Travel time between docks `a` and `b` is the Manhattan distance

`dist(a,b) = |x_a - x_b| + |y_a - y_b|`.

Boarding and unloading take zero time.

A person is considered **evacuated** when they are unloaded at dock `1`.  
You may visit docks in any order and may board or unload any subset of eligible people, as long as the ferry capacity constraints are never violated.

Your task is to output **any feasible evacuation plan** that minimizes the total weighted arrival time.

---

## Input

The input describes one test instance.

- First line: `m n K C`
- Next `m` lines: two integers `x_j y_j` — coordinates of dock `j`
- Next `n` lines: three integers `s_i w_i u_i` — initial dock, weight, and urgency of person `i`

People are indexed in the order they appear in the input, from `1` to `n`.

---

## Output

Output a sequence of actions.

- First line: integer `q` — number of actions
- Next `q` lines: each action must be one of the following:

### Move
`MOVE v`

Move the ferry from its current dock to dock `v` (`1 <= v <= m`, `v` must differ from the current dock).

### Board
`BOARD t id_1 id_2 ... id_t`

At the current dock, board exactly these `t` people onto the ferry.

Requirements:

- every listed person must currently be waiting at this dock,
- no listed person may have been boarded before,
- after boarding, the ferry must still satisfy both capacity constraints.

### Unload
`UNLOAD t id_1 id_2 ... id_t`

At dock `1`, unload exactly these `t` people from the ferry.

For each unloaded person, their evacuation time is the current time.

---

### Feasibility requirements

Your output is **feasible** iff all of the following hold:

1. The action sequence is syntactically valid.
2. The ferry starts at dock `1` at time `0`, empty.
3. Every `MOVE` updates time by the correct Manhattan distance.
4. Every `BOARD` and `UNLOAD` action is valid at the current dock.
5. At all times, the ferry carries at most `C` people and total onboard weight at most `K`.
6. Every person is boarded exactly once.
7. Every person is unloaded exactly once at dock `1`.
8. After the last action, all people have been evacuated and the ferry is empty.
9. `q <= 300000`.

If any condition is violated, the solution is infeasible.

---

## Objective

For each person `i`, let `T_i` be the time when that person is unloaded at dock `1`.

Minimize

`F = sum(u_i * T_i)` over all people `i = 1..n`.

If the output is infeasible, it has no valid objective value and receives score `0`.

---

## Scoring

Each test file is scored independently.

Let:

- `Y` = your objective value on that test,
- `B` = the objective value of the **baseline algorithm** defined below on the same test.

If your output is infeasible, the score for that test is `0`.

Otherwise,

`score = min(200, 100 * B / Y)`

So:

- matching the baseline gives score `100`,
- improving on the baseline gives more than `100`,
- doing worse than the baseline gives less than `100`.

The final score is the arithmetic mean of per-test scores over all official test files.

### Baseline algorithm

The baseline is deterministic.

Maintain, for each dock `j >= 2`, the list of people still waiting there, sorted by:

1. larger urgency `u_i` first,
2. smaller person index first.

A baseline trip always visits exactly one non-shelter dock and then returns immediately to dock `1`.

At the start of each trip:

1. For every non-empty dock `j`:
   - Scan its sorted waiting list from the front.
   - Build a prefix `P_j` by taking people one by one until taking the next person would violate either:
     - total weight `<= K`, or
     - passenger count `<= C`.
   - Let `value_j = sum(u_i)` over people in `P_j`.
   - Let `cost_j = 2 * dist(1, j)`.
   - Let `ratio_j = value_j / cost_j`.

2. Choose the dock `j` with maximum `ratio_j`.
   - Compare ratios exactly by cross multiplication.
   - Break ties by smaller dock index.

3. Sail from dock `1` to dock `j`, board all people in `P_j`, return directly to dock `1`, and unload them.

Repeat until everyone is evacuated.

---

## Constraints

- `2 <= m <= 80`
- `1 <= n <= 500`
- `1 <= C <= 25`
- `1 <= K <= 5000`
- `0 <= x_j, y_j <= 10000`
- `2 <= s_i <= m`
- `1 <= w_i <= K`
- `1 <= u_i <= 1000000`

All input values are integers.

- Time limit: 2 seconds
- Memory limit: 256 MB

---

## Example

### Input
```text
4 3 150 2
0 0
2 0
2 2
5 0
2 50 10
3 50 8
4 100 7
```

### Output
```text
10
MOVE 2
BOARD 1 1
MOVE 3
BOARD 1 2
MOVE 1
UNLOAD 2 1 2
MOVE 4
BOARD 1 3
MOVE 1
UNLOAD 1 3
```

### Explanation

The ferry first picks up person `1` at dock `2`, then person `2` at dock `3`, and returns to the shelter.

- `dist(1,2)=2`
- `dist(2,3)=2`
- `dist(3,1)=4`

So persons `1` and `2` arrive at time `8`.

Then it serves dock `4`:

- `dist(1,4)=5`
- `dist(4,1)=5`

So person `3` arrives at time `18`.

Objective:

`F = 10*8 + 8*8 + 7*18 = 270`

For this instance, the baseline makes one-dock round trips and gets objective `290`, so this output would score

`100 * 290 / 270 = 107.407...`
