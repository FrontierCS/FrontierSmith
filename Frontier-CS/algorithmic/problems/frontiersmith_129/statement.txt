# Sparse Alloy Sourcing

## Problem

A manufacturer must fulfill many customer contracts for iron–aluminum–tin alloys.

Each **supplier** offers a single raw stream with a fixed composition:
- iron fraction,
- aluminum fraction,
- tin fraction.

For each supplier `i`:
- if you buy any positive amount from that supplier, you pay a **setup cost** `F_i`,
- you then pay a **variable cost** `C_i` per ton purchased,
- if the supplier is used at all, the total purchased amount must be between `L_i` and `U_i` tons.

Each **contract** `j` requires exactly `Q_j` tons of alloy with an exact target composition:
- iron fraction `x_j`,
- aluminum fraction `y_j`,
- tin fraction `z_j`.

You may split any supplier’s material across many contracts.  
Each contract may use material from **at most 4 suppliers**.

Your task is to output a feasible sourcing-and-blending plan with minimum total cost.

To guarantee feasibility, the first three suppliers are always:
1. pure iron `(1, 0, 0)`,
2. pure aluminum `(0, 1, 0)`,
3. pure tin `(0, 0, 1)`,

with `L_i = 0` and sufficiently large `U_i`.

---

## Input

The first line contains two integers:

`m n` — number of suppliers and number of contracts.

The next `m` lines each contain 7 real numbers describing one supplier:

`a_i b_i c_i C_i F_i L_i U_i`

where:
- `a_i, b_i, c_i` are the iron / aluminum / tin fractions,
- `C_i` is variable cost per ton,
- `F_i` is setup cost,
- `L_i` is minimum total purchase if used,
- `U_i` is maximum total purchase.

The next `n` lines each contain 4 real numbers describing one contract:

`x_j y_j z_j Q_j`

where:
- `x_j, y_j, z_j` are the required iron / aluminum / tin fractions,
- `Q_j` is the required tonnage.

All composition triples sum to exactly `1` in the input.

---

## Output

Output a sparse blending plan.

The first line must contain one integer:

`R` — the number of nonzero `(contract, supplier, amount)` assignments.

Each of the next `R` lines must contain:

`j i t`

meaning: use `t` tons of supplier `i` in contract `j`.

### Feasibility requirements

Let `y_{j,i}` be the total amount assigned from supplier `i` to contract `j` after summing duplicate pairs, if any.

Your output is **feasible** iff all of the following hold:

1. `1 <= j <= n`, `1 <= i <= m`, and `t > 0` for every listed assignment.
2. For every contract `j`, at most **4** suppliers have positive `y_{j,i}`.
3. For every contract `j`:
   - total mass matches:
     \[
     \sum_i y_{j,i} = Q_j
     \]
   - iron matches:
     \[
     \sum_i a_i y_{j,i} = x_j Q_j
     \]
   - aluminum matches:
     \[
     \sum_i b_i y_{j,i} = y_j Q_j
     \]
   - tin matches:
     \[
     \sum_i c_i y_{j,i} = z_j Q_j
     \]
4. For every supplier `i`, let
   \[
   X_i = \sum_j y_{j,i}
   \]
   Then either:
   - `X_i = 0`, or
   - `L_i <= X_i <= U_i`.

For floating-point checking, the judge uses absolute tolerance

\[
\varepsilon = 10^{-6} \cdot \max(1, Q_j)
\]

for each contract balance/composition equation, and

\[
10^{-6} \cdot \max(1, U_i)
\]

for supplier bounds.

If your output is infeasible, its score for that test case is `0`.

---

## Objective

Minimize the total sourcing cost:

\[
\text{Cost} = \sum_{i : X_i > 0} \left(F_i + C_i X_i\right)
\]

where `X_i` is the total amount purchased from supplier `i`.

---

## Scoring

For each test case:

- Let `C` be your cost if your output is feasible.
- Let `B` be the **baseline cost**, defined as using only the first three suppliers (pure iron, pure aluminum, pure tin).

Since those suppliers are pure, the baseline is:

\[
D_{\text{Fe}} = \sum_j x_j Q_j,\quad
D_{\text{Al}} = \sum_j y_j Q_j,\quad
D_{\text{Sn}} = \sum_j z_j Q_j
\]

\[
B =
[\!D_{\text{Fe}}>0\!]\,(F_1 + C_1 D_{\text{Fe}})
+
[\!D_{\text{Al}}>0\!]\,(F_2 + C_2 D_{\text{Al}})
+
[\!D_{\text{Sn}}>0\!]\,(F_3 + C_3 D_{\text{Sn}})
\]

where `[...]` is `1` if the condition is true, otherwise `0`.

The test-case score is:

\[
\text{score} = 10^6 \cdot \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{B}{C},\,0,\,2\right)
\]

If your output is infeasible, `score = 0`.

The final score is the arithmetic mean of test-case scores.

Thus:
- matching the baseline gives `1,000,000`,
- beating the baseline gives more than `1,000,000`,
- halving the baseline cost reaches the cap `2,000,000`,
- worse solutions still receive partial credit.

---

## Constraints

- `1 <= m <= 300`
- `1 <= n <= 500`
- all input numbers have at most 6 digits after the decimal point
- `0 <= a_i, b_i, c_i, x_j, y_j, z_j <= 1`
- `a_i + b_i + c_i = 1`
- `x_j + y_j + z_j = 1`
- `0 <= L_i <= U_i`
- `0 <= C_i, F_i <= 10^6`
- `0 < Q_j <= 10^6`

Additional guarantees:
- supplier `1` is `(1,0,0)`,
- supplier `2` is `(0,1,0)`,
- supplier `3` is `(0,0,1)`,
- suppliers `1..3` have `L_i = 0`,
- suppliers `1..3` have enough `U_i` to satisfy all demand.

- Time limit: 2 seconds
- Memory limit: 512 MB

---

## Example

### Sample Input
```text
5 2
1 0 0 10 5 0 100
0 1 0 12 5 0 100
0 0 1 20 5 0 100
0.5 0.5 0 8 10 2 10
0.2 0.3 0.5 9 8 1 10
0.4 0.4 0.2 5
0.2 0.3 0.5 4
```

### Sample Output
```text
4
1 4 2.8
1 5 2
1 1 0.2
2 5 4
```

### Explanation

- Contract 1 gets:
  - `2.8` tons from supplier 4,
  - `2` tons from supplier 5,
  - `0.2` tons of pure iron.
- Contract 2 gets:
  - `4` tons from supplier 5.

This is feasible:
- each contract uses at most 4 suppliers,
- both target compositions are matched exactly,
- supplier 4 is used for `2.8` tons, satisfying `2 <= X_4 <= 10`,
- supplier 5 is used for `6` tons, satisfying `1 <= X_5 <= 10`.

Cost:
- supplier 1: `5 + 10 * 0.2 = 7`
- supplier 4: `10 + 8 * 2.8 = 32.4`
- supplier 5: `8 + 9 * 6 = 62`

Total:
\[
C = 101.4
\]

Baseline (pure metals only):
- total iron demand = `2.8`
- total aluminum demand = `3.2`
- total tin demand = `3.0`

\[
B = (5 + 10 \cdot 2.8) + (5 + 12 \cdot 3.2) + (5 + 20 \cdot 3) = 141.4
\]

So the sample score for this single test case is:

\[
10^6 \cdot \frac{141.4}{101.4} \approx 1{,}394{,}477
\]

(ignoring final rounding details).
