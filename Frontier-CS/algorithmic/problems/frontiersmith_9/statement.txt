# Yae Village Defense Network

## Problem

The ancient village of Yae has `n` houses, numbered `1..n`. House `1` is the Grand Shrine, where Sakura starts and ends every day.

There are `m` candidate bidirectional roads. Road `i` connects two houses `u_i, v_i`, has:

- build cost `c_i`
- travel time `d_i`

Before the first day begins, Sakura may choose a subset of roads to build, with total build cost at most `B`. Built roads remain available for all days.

Over `D` days, Collapse beasts will appear. Attack `j` is described by:

- house `h_j`
- release day `a_j`
- deadline day `b_j` (`a_j <= b_j`)
- energy `w_j`

An attack is **active** on every day `t` with `a_j <= t <= b_j`. If Sakura visits `h_j` on any active day, that attack is cleared and causes no damage. Otherwise, if it is still uncleared after day `b_j`, it causes damage `w_j`.

On each day `t`, Sakura starts at house `1`, walks along built roads, and must return to house `1` by the end of the day. The total travel time used that day must not exceed `L_t`.

If Sakura visits a house on a day, then **all attacks active at that house on that day** are cleared immediately.

Your task is to output:

1. which roads to build;
2. one patrol walk for each day;

so as to minimize total damage from uncleared attacks.

This is an optimization problem: many outputs may be feasible, and better outputs receive higher scores.

---

## Input

The input describes one instance.

```text
n m D A B
u_1 v_1 c_1 d_1
u_2 v_2 c_2 d_2
...
u_m v_m c_m d_m
L_1 L_2 ... L_D
h_1 a_1 b_1 w_1
h_2 a_2 b_2 w_2
...
h_A a_A b_A w_A
```

### Meaning

- `n`: number of houses
- `m`: number of candidate roads
- `D`: number of days
- `A`: number of attacks
- `B`: total road-building budget

For each road `i`:

- `u_i, v_i`: endpoints
- `c_i`: build cost
- `d_i`: travel time

For each day `t`:

- `L_t`: maximum total travel time allowed on day `t`

For each attack `j`:

- `h_j`: attacked house
- `a_j`: release day
- `b_j`: deadline day
- `w_j`: damage if not cleared in time

All roads are bidirectional. Multiple roads between the same pair of houses may exist.

---

## Output

Your output must have exactly `D + 1` lines.

### Line 1: built roads

```text
k e_1 e_2 ... e_k
```

- `k` is the number of roads you build.
- `e_1, ..., e_k` are distinct road indices in `[1, m]`.

Their total build cost must satisfy:

```text
sum(c_{e_i}) <= B
```

### Lines 2 to D+1: daily patrols

For each day `t = 1..D`, output one line:

```text
p_t f_{t,1} f_{t,2} ... f_{t,p_t}
```

- `p_t` is the number of road traversals on day `t`
- each `f_{t,s}` is a built road index

The patrol is interpreted as follows:

- Sakura starts at house `1`
- for each listed road index, she traverses that road
- at every step, the chosen road must be incident to Sakura’s current house
- traversing road `i` moves Sakura to its other endpoint
- after all `p_t` traversals, Sakura must be back at house `1`

The total travel time on day `t` is:

```text
sum(d_{f_{t,s}})
```

and must satisfy:

```text
sum(d_{f_{t,s}}) <= L_t
```

`p_t = 0` is allowed; this means Sakura stays at house `1` all day.

### Feasibility requirements

A submission is **feasible** iff all of the following hold:

1. all road indices are valid;
2. built road indices are distinct;
3. total build cost does not exceed `B`;
4. every traversed road was built;
5. each daily patrol starts at house `1`, follows valid adjacent roads, and ends at house `1`;
6. each daily patrol respects that day's travel limit.

If any condition fails, the submission is infeasible.

---

## Objective

For each attack `j`, let it be **cleared** if there exists a day `t` such that:

- `a_j <= t <= b_j`, and
- house `h_j` is visited by Sakura on day `t`

A house is considered visited on a day if Sakura is there at the start of the day, after any traversal, or at the end of the day.

The objective value is the total damage:

```text
Damage = sum(w_j for all attacks j that are not cleared)
```

Your goal is to **minimize** `Damage`.

If your output is infeasible, it is treated as having infinite damage.

---

## Scoring

For each test file, define the **baseline** solution as:

- build no roads;
- on every day, output `0` (stay at house `1`).

Let `BaseDamage` be the damage of that baseline on the test file.

Let your feasible damage be `YourDamage`.

The score for one test file is:

```text
Score = floor(1,000,000 * min(10, (BaseDamage + 1) / (YourDamage + 1)))
```

If your output is infeasible, the score for that test file is `0`.

- The baseline always scores exactly `1,000,000`.
- Better solutions score more than `1,000,000`.
- Worse feasible solutions score less than `1,000,000`.

Your final score is the arithmetic mean of the per-file scores over all official test files.

---

## Constraints

- `2 <= n <= 400`
- `1 <= m <= 3000`
- `1 <= D <= 60`
- `1 <= A <= 4000`
- `1 <= u_i, v_i <= n`
- `1 <= c_i <= 10^6`
- `1 <= d_i <= 10^6`
- `1 <= B <= 10^9`
- `0 <= L_t <= 10^9`
- `1 <= h_j <= n`
- `1 <= a_j <= b_j <= D`
- `1 <= w_j <= 10^12`

The total damage fits in signed 64-bit integer.

### Notes

- All attack information is known in advance.
- There may be days with `L_t = 0`.
- Attacks at house `1` can be cleared even by staying at the shrine.
- Exact optimization is expected to be hard; heuristic methods are encouraged.

- Time limit: 3 seconds
- Memory limit: 256 MB

---

## Example

### Sample Input

```text
4 4 3 4 4
1 2 2 2
2 3 2 2
1 4 4 3
3 4 3 2
4 8 0
2 1 2 5
3 2 3 8
4 1 1 6
1 3 3 4
```

### Sample Output

```text
2 1 2
2 1 1
4 1 2 2 1
0
```

### Explanation

Built roads:

- road 1: `1-2`, cost 2, time 2
- road 2: `2-3`, cost 2, time 2

Total build cost is `4`, within budget.

Patrols:

- Day 1: traverse road 1 out and back, visiting house 2
- Day 2: traverse `1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 2 -> 1`, visiting house 3
- Day 3: stay at house 1

This clears:

- attack at house 2, days 1..2, energy 5
- attack at house 3, days 2..3, energy 8
- attack at house 1, day 3, energy 4

The attack at house 4 on day 1 is missed, so:

```text
Damage = 6
```

The baseline builds no roads and only clears attacks at house 1, so:

```text
BaseDamage = 5 + 8 + 6 = 19
```

Thus the sample output would receive:

```text
floor(1,000,000 * min(10, (19 + 1) / (6 + 1))) = 2,857,142
```
