# Chamber Re-Seating

## Problem

After a week of chaotic debates, the parliament will physically re-seat all members before the next session.

There are `n` members of parliament, numbered `1..n`. On each of the five weekdays, a journalist photographed some unordered pairs of members who were visibly arguing. If a pair appears on multiple days, it is considered a stronger conflict.

The chamber has exactly two wings:

- **Wing A** with `ceil(n / 2)` seats,
- **Wing B** with `floor(n / 2)` seats.

Within each wing, seats are arranged in a single line from **front** to **back**, numbered starting from `1`.

You must output a **permutation** of the members. The first `ceil(n / 2)` members in that permutation will be seated in Wing A from front to back, and the remaining members will be seated in Wing B from front to back.

Your goal is to minimize the total expected disturbance in future debates.

Only the pairs that appear in the five photos matter.

---

### Disturbance model

For each unordered pair `{u, v}`:

- let `w(u, v)` be the number of weekdays on which `{u, v}` appears in the photos (`1..5`);
- let `row(u)` and `row(v)` be their row numbers within their wings;
- let `sameWing(u, v)` indicate whether they are seated in the same wing.

Their disturbance contribution is:

- if they are in the **same wing**:
  
  `w(u, v) * (20 + max(0, 15 - |row(u) - row(v)|))`

- if they are in **different wings**:
  
  `w(u, v) * max(0, 14 - |row(u) - row(v)|)`

So:
- placing a frequently arguing pair in the same wing is always expensive;
- even across wings, putting such a pair on nearby rows is still bad;
- putting a pair far apart reduces the penalty.

The total objective is the sum of these contributions over all photographed pairs.

Finding the true optimum is expected to be computationally hard; this is an optimization challenge.

## Input

The input consists of 6 lines.

- Line 1: integer `n` — the number of members.
- Lines 2..6: one line for each weekday.

Each weekday line has the format:

`p u1 v1 u2 v2 ... up vp`

where:

- `p` is the number of photographed arguing pairs that day,
- each `(ui, vi)` is an unordered pair of distinct members.

Pairs on the same day are guaranteed to be distinct.  
The same pair may appear on multiple different days.

All whitespace should be treated as standard whitespace.

## Output

Output one line containing a permutation of `1..n`:

`a1 a2 ... an`

Interpretation:

- `a1, a2, ..., aceil(n/2)` are seated in Wing A, rows `1..ceil(n/2)`,
- `a(ceil(n/2)+1), ..., an` are seated in Wing B, rows `1..floor(n/2)`.

### Feasibility requirements

Your output is **feasible** iff:

- it contains exactly `n` integers,
- each integer is between `1` and `n`,
- every member appears exactly once.

If the output is infeasible, the score for that test case is `0`.

## Objective

Let `P` be the total disturbance penalty of your seating plan, computed as described above.

Your goal is to **minimize `P`**.

## Scoring

For each test case, let:

- `P` = your penalty,
- `P_base` = the penalty of the baseline seating

`1 2 3 ... n`

(i.e. members `1..ceil(n/2)` in Wing A, the rest in Wing B).

The score for the test case is:

`floor(500000 * (2 * P_base + 1) / (P + P_base + 1))`

Properties:

- baseline always gets exactly `500000`,
- better solutions get more than `500000`,
- worse solutions get less than `500000`,
- the score approaches `1000000` as `P` approaches `0`.

The final score is the arithmetic mean of the per-test-case scores, rounded down to the nearest integer.

## Constraints

- `1 <= n <= 200000`
- `0 <= p <= 200000` on each day
- total number of listed pairs over all 5 days is at most `500000`
- `1 <= ui, vi <= n`
- `ui != vi`

## Constraints
- Time limit: 4 seconds
- Memory limit: 512 MB

## Example

### Sample input
```text
4
1 1 2
1 1 3
1 2 4
1 3 4
0
```

### Sample output
```text
1 4 2 3
```

### Explanation

Wing A has seats `[1, 4]`, Wing B has seats `[2, 3]`.

The photographed pairs are:

- `{1,2}` once
- `{1,3}` once
- `{2,4}` once
- `{3,4}` once

For this seating:

- `1` and `2` are in different wings, same row: penalty `14`
- `1` and `3` are in different wings, row difference `1`: penalty `13`
- `2` and `4` are in different wings, row difference `1`: penalty `13`
- `3` and `4` are in different wings, same row: penalty `14`

So `P = 54`.

The baseline seating is `1 2 3 4`, whose penalty is `96`, so this output scores above the baseline.
