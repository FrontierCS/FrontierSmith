```markdown
# Perpetual Caravan Coverage

## Problem

A trade league wants to keep long-term contracts active across a road network.

There are `n` cities and `m` one-way roads. City `i` has contract value `w_i`.

The league may launch several independent caravans. Each caravan:

- starts in one chosen city,
- receives some integer amount of initial capital,
- follows a finite **plan** consisting of a prefix and then a directed cycle,
- repeats that cycle forever.

A road can be used only if the caravan currently has enough capital. After taking a road, the caravan's capital changes by that road's profit/loss.

Your task is to design up to `K` perpetual caravan plans, with total initial capital at most `B`, to **maximize the total contract value of cities visited by at least one caravan**.

A city is considered covered if at least one caravan visits it at least once, either in its prefix or in one traversal of its cycle.

---

For each road `e`:

- it goes from city `u_e` to city `v_e`,
- it requires current capital at least `r_e` before traversal,
- after traversal, capital becomes `capital + p_e`.

Here `p_e` may be negative.

A caravan plan is valid if:

1. its listed roads form a continuous walk starting from its chosen start city,
2. after the designated prefix, the remaining roads form a directed cycle,
3. at every traversal in the listed walk, the caravan has enough capital and its capital never becomes negative after the move,
4. after one full traversal of the cycle, the caravan ends with capital **at least** what it had at the start of the cycle.

Condition 4 guarantees the cycle can be repeated forever.

All caravans are independent: they do not share capital, roads, or cities in any special way.

## Input

The input describes one test file.

- The first line contains four integers  
  `n m K B`
- The second line contains `n` integers  
  `w_1 w_2 ... w_n`
- The next `m` lines each contain four integers  
  `u_e v_e r_e p_e`  
  describing road `e` (roads are numbered from `1` to `m` in input order)

## Output

On the first line, output one integer `q` — the number of caravans you launch.

Then output `q` caravan descriptions.

For each caravan, output:

- one line with four integers  
  `s A L z`
  - `s` = start city
  - `A` = initial capital
  - `L` = number of roads in the listed walk
  - `z` = number of roads in the prefix

- one line with `L` integers  
  `e_1 e_2 ... e_L`  
  the road IDs used, in order

The meaning is:

- the caravan starts at city `s` with capital `A`,
- it traverses roads `e_1, e_2, ..., e_L`,
- the first `z` roads are the prefix,
- the remaining `L - z` roads are the cycle repeated forever.

### Feasibility requirements

Your output is feasible iff all of the following hold:

- `0 <= q <= K`
- for every caravan:
  - `0 <= A`
  - `1 <= L`
  - `0 <= z < L`  
    (the cycle must contain at least one road)
  - road `e_1` starts at city `s`
  - for all `t` from `1` to `L-1`, road `e_t` ends where road `e_{t+1}` starts
  - if `z = 0`, then after road `e_L` the caravan returns to city `s`
  - if `z > 0`, then after road `e_L` the caravan returns to the city reached after road `e_z`
  - during simulation of roads `e_1..e_L`:
    - before traversing road `e_t`, current capital must be at least that road's `r`
    - after traversing it, current capital changes by `p`
    - current capital must remain at least `0`
  - if `C_cycle` is the capital immediately after the first `z` roads, and `C_end` is the capital after all `L` roads, then `C_end >= C_cycle`
- the sum of all caravans' initial capitals is at most `B`
- the sum of all `L` values is at most `400000`

If the output is infeasible, the score for that test file is `0`.

## Objective

Let a city be **covered** if it is visited by at least one caravan at one of these moments:

- its start city,
- the city reached after any traversed road in the listed walk.

(Only the listed walk matters; repeating the same cycle forever does not add new cities beyond those already seen in one cycle traversal.)

Let `S` be the set of covered cities.

Your objective value is

\[
O = \sum_{i \in S} w_i
\]

Maximize `O`.

## Scoring

For a test file:

- let `W = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i`
- the official baseline submits `q = 0`, so its objective is `O_base = 0`

Your per-file score is

\[
\text{score} =
100 \times \frac{\max(0, O - O_{base})}{W - O_{base}}
=
100 \times \frac{O}{W}
\]

clamped to the range `[0, 100]`.

If your output is infeasible, the per-file score is `0`.

The final score is the arithmetic mean of per-file scores over all test files.

## Constraints

- `2 <= n, m <= 200000`
- `1 <= K <= 60`
- `0 <= B <= 10^{12}`
- `1 <= w_i <= 10^9`
- `1 <= u_e, v_e <= n`
- `u_e != v_e`
- `0 <= r_e <= 10^9`
- `-10^9 <= p_e <= 10^9`
- total output walk length must be at most `400000`

- Time limit: 4 seconds
- Memory limit: 512 MB

## Example

### Sample Input
```text
5 6 2 4
5 4 7 6 3
1 2 2 1
2 3 3 0
3 1 3 1
4 5 1 0
5 4 1 0
2 4 10 5
```

### Sample Output
```text
2
1 3 3 0
1 2 3
4 1 2 0
4 5
```

### Explanation

- Caravan 1 starts at city `1` with capital `3`, and repeats roads `1 -> 2 -> 3`.
  - Capitals: `3 -> 4 -> 4 -> 5`
  - It returns to city `1`, and ends the cycle with at least its starting cycle capital, so it can repeat forever.
  - It covers cities `1, 2, 3`.

- Caravan 2 starts at city `4` with capital `1`, and repeats roads `4 -> 5`.
  - Capitals: `1 -> 1 -> 1`
  - It covers cities `4, 5`.

All 5 cities are covered, so

\[
O = 5 + 4 + 7 + 6 + 3 = 25
\]

and since `W = 25`, the score for this file is `100`.
