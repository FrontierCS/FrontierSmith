# Arcane Marshalling Yard

## Problem

A long inbound train arrives at a magical marshalling yard. Its cars stand on a single main track in a fixed front-to-back order. Each car has:

- a unique **serial number**,
- a required **destination train**,
- a **value** earned if that car is successfully shipped.

The yard also has several one-ended sidings. A siding behaves like a **stack**: cars may only be taken from or added to its exposed end.

Outbound destination trains must depart in the fixed order `1, 2, ..., T`. At any moment exactly one destination train is **current**. You may only ship cars onto the current destination train, and within each destination train the shipped cars must appear in **strictly increasing serial number** order.

You may manipulate cars using the following operations:

- move the accessible car from the inbound track or from the top of one siding to the top of another siding,
- ship the accessible car from the inbound track or from the top of one siding to the current destination train,
- discard the accessible car from the inbound track or from the top of one siding,
- close the current destination train and advance to the next one.

Your task is to produce any feasible sequence of operations with a high objective value.

This is an optimization problem: better plans earn higher scores.

## Input

The first line contains three integers:

`n S T` — number of cars, number of sidings, number of destination trains.

The second line contains `S` integers:

`c1 c2 ... cS` — capacities of the sidings.

The next `n` lines describe the inbound cars in **front-to-back** order.  
Line `i` contains three integers:

`serial_i dest_i value_i`

where:

- `serial_i` is the unique serial number of the car,
- `dest_i` is its required destination train,
- `value_i` is the value earned if the car is shipped.

## Output

On the first line print an integer `q` — the number of operations.

Then print `q` lines, each describing one operation in one of the following formats:

- `H a b`  
  Take the accessible car from source `a` and push it onto the top of siding `b`.

- `D a`  
  Take the accessible car from source `a` and ship it to the current destination train.

- `X a`  
  Take the accessible car from source `a` and discard it.

- `N`  
  Close the current destination train and advance to the next one.

### Sources

- `0` means the inbound track; its accessible car is its current front car.
- `1..S` mean sidings; their accessible car is the top car of that siding.

### Feasibility rules

A submission is feasible iff all of the following hold:

1. Every operation is valid when executed in order.
2. For `H a b`:
   - `a` must be a non-empty source,
   - `b` must be a siding index in `1..S`,
   - `a != b`,
   - siding `b` must not be full.
3. For `D a`:
   - `a` must be a non-empty source,
   - there must still be a current destination train (`current <= T`),
   - the moved car must have `dest = current`,
   - its serial number must be strictly larger than the serial number of the last car already shipped to the current destination train.
4. For `X a`:
   - `a` must be a non-empty source.
5. For `N`:
   - there must still be a current destination train (`current <= T`).
6. After all `q` operations, the inbound track and every siding must be empty.
7. `q` must not exceed `2,000,000`.

Empty destination trains are allowed.

## Objective

Let:

- `V` = sum of `value_i` over all cars shipped using `D`,
- `M` = number of operations of types `H` and `D`.

The objective value of a feasible submission is:

`OBJ = V - M`

Discard operations `X` and train-advance operations `N` do **not** directly cost anything.

If the output is infeasible, the submission receives score `0`.

## Scoring

For each test case, define:

- `U = sum(value_i)` over all cars in the input,
- `B` = the objective value of the following deterministic baseline algorithm.

### Baseline algorithm

The baseline uses **no sidings**.

- Start with current destination train `cur = 1`.
- Let `last = 0`, meaning the last serial shipped to train `cur`.
- Scan the inbound cars from front to back.
- For each car `(serial, dest, value)`:
  - if `dest < cur`, discard it;
  - if `dest > cur`, repeatedly close trains until `cur = dest`, then set `last = 0`;
  - now `dest = cur`:
    - if `serial > last`, ship it directly and add `value - 1` to the baseline objective; set `last = serial`;
    - otherwise discard it.

The per-test score is

`score = 1,000,000 * clamp((OBJ - B) / (U - B), 0, 1)`

where `clamp(x, 0, 1) = max(0, min(1, x))`.

If `U = B`, every feasible submission for that test receives `1,000,000`.

The final score is the arithmetic mean of the per-test scores over all test cases.

## Constraints

- `1 <= n <= 100000`
- `1 <= S <= 30`
- `1 <= T <= 100000`
- `1 <= c_j <= n`
- `serial_i` are distinct and form a permutation of `1..n`
- `1 <= dest_i <= T`
- `1 <= value_i <= 10^9`
- Time limit: 2 seconds
- Memory limit: 256 MB

## Example

### Input
```text
6 2 3
2 3
4 2 8
1 1 7
2 1 6
6 3 5
3 2 9
5 2 4
```

### Output
```text
10
H 0 1
D 0
D 0
N
D 1
H 0 2
X 0
D 0
N
D 2
```

### Explanation

A high-level view of the plan:

- Store car `(4,2,8)` in siding 1.
- Ship cars `1` and `2` to destination train 1.
- Advance to destination train 2.
- Recover and ship car `4`.
- Store car `6` for destination train 3 in siding 2.
- Discard car `3` because serial `3` cannot follow already shipped serial `4` on destination train 2.
- Ship car `5` to destination train 2.
- Advance to destination train 3.
- Ship car `6`.

Delivered value: `7 + 6 + 8 + 4 + 5 = 30`  
Counted moves (`H` and `D`): `7`  
So `OBJ = 30 - 7 = 23`.

For this instance, the baseline objective is `11`, and `U = 39`, so this output scores

`1,000,000 * (23 - 11) / (39 - 11) = 428571.428...`
